Three logo directions for SmileAI

SmileAI helps people in Libya check their teeth with AI, understand the result, and book a clinic. We studied the app, the market and today’s design trends, then designed three different logos. All three use the app’s own colors and fonts — so any of them can go live without changing the look of the app.

01 · The brief

First, we studied the app

The big finding

In Arabic calligraphy, the three small spikes of the letter س are called its “teeth” (أسنان السين).

And س is the first letter of سمايل (SmileAI) and of سِنّ (tooth). A letter that already has teeth — with a smile-shaped curve under them. Concept 01 is built on this.

The idea

We drew the letter س as one rounded stroke. Its three spikes are the teeth. Its round bowl is the smile.

Arabic readers see the first letter of سمايل. Everyone else sees a happy, toothy smile. Both readings are true — and no other dental app can own this shape.

  • Ink teeth + blue smile: the two colors tell the story.
  • One stroke weight and rounded ends — the same soft feel as Poppins and Tajawal in the app.
  • In the Arabic wordmark the bowl of the last letter ل is blue too: the word ends with a smile.

The idea

Dentists draw your mouth from above as a U-shaped chart. We kept only the dots — and the chart became a smile.

The dots grow from small front teeth to large molars, like a real dental arch. One dot is blue: the finding. That is how SmileAI works — the AI looks at every tooth and shows you the one that needs attention.

  • Eight dots: incisors, canines, premolars and molars.
  • In the app, the blue dot can move to point at a real finding.
  • Motion: the dots appear one by one, like a scan.

The idea

The AI reads your photo line by line. We drew the tooth the same way: seven scan lines, and one blue line where the scan is right now.

It is a picture, not a letter — anyone, in any language, sees a tooth at once. The lines add “digital” and “precise” without the usual AI clichés. In the app, the blue line can move down the tooth while the AI is working.

  • The outline comes from a real molar: two cusps on top, two roots below.
  • Round line ends match the soft feel of Poppins and Tajawal.
  • Motion: the lines draw in, then the blue line scans down.

What we found

The blue is perfect for shapes, not for small text. #3A9BE0 has 3.0:1 contrast with white. That is fine for the logo, icons and big titles, but too low for normal text (the rule is 4.5:1).

Fix for buttons: Blue 600. For buttons with white text, use #0A7CBF — the same blue, just a little deeper (4.5:1). People will not notice the change, but more people can read the button.

For links, use the app’s own Blue 700. #1C6FA8 is already in the code (primaryDark) and passes with 5.4:1.

Status colors need a word. The green, amber and orange are 2.3–3.2:1 on white. Always show them with an icon and a label (Good, Attention, See a dentist) — never color alone.

The hint grey is too light. #9AA5AF is 2.5:1. Keep it for placeholders only; use #66707B (5.0:1) for helper text.

Blue is common, so the shape must do the work. Many dental and health apps are blue. Because we keep the app’s blue, each concept puts its difference in the shape — the letter, the chart, the scan lines.

08 · A note on the name

Keep SmileAI — or go further?

You told us you are open to a new name. SmileAI is clear and easy in both languages, but it is generic: other apps and companies already use “Smile AI” (for example an App Store app called “Smile AI: Improve Your Teeth”, and Smiles.ai in India). This can make search and trademarks harder.

SmileAI · سمايل

Keep it. Lowest cost, and users already know it. Show the Arabic سمايل everywhere so it feels local.

Safe choice
Basma · بسمة

“Smile” in Arabic. Warm, short and easy to say in English. Concepts 02 and 03 work with it as they are. It could also be “Basma AI”.

Most distinctive
Sinn · سِنّ

“Tooth” in Arabic, and it fits Concept 01. But in English it sounds like “sin”, so we do not recommend it.

Not recommended

Before any name change, check the trademark and the name on the App Store and Google Play in Libya and the region.
